The open-data hub on strategic minerals, oil and gas and the green transition

Why it matters

Small markets, outsized leverage

Strategic minerals are tiny markets compared with oil or iron ore, but everything downstream depends on them. Concentration in mining and, above all, refining makes them a lever of industrial and geopolitical power.

Data & methodology

Built on open, verifiable sources

Every dashboard cross-references primary open datasets. Figures are cited to their source and dated; estimates are flagged as such.

The data is open too. The open data catalogue lists every dataset behind the site — 110 BGS commodity series as JSON plus the curated dashboard data, all under CC BY-NC-SA — and the Mineral Explorer maps production, imports and exports for 111 commodities and 190 countries, 1970–2024.

  • USGS National Minerals Information Center — Mineral Commodity Summaries: world production, reserves and US trade statistics, per commodity.
  • British Geological Survey — World Mineral Statistics, long-run production series from 1970.
  • UN Comtrade — bilateral trade flows by HS code, for mapping who ships to whom.
  • IEA Critical Minerals — demand scenarios, supply-chain analysis and price context.
  • World Bank WITS — accessible interface over Comtrade data.
  • Method notes — map locations are approximate; trade arcs indicate major corridors, not exact tonnages; downstream (battery-grade) shares are estimates where official statistics do not separate them. Each page states its last data refresh.

FAQ

Common questions

What is mineral.watch?

mineral.watch is an independent open data and intelligence portal on the global mineral, oil and gas, and green-transition industries. It cross-references verified data from USGS, BGS, UN Comtrade, the IEA, the EIA and OPEC to show who produces, refines, ships and depends on the materials and fuels behind batteries, magnets, chips, grids and defence systems — and publishes the underlying datasets openly.

Which minerals and industries does mineral.watch cover?

Thirteen mineral dashboards are live — graphite, rare earths, copper, uranium, lithium, manganese, cobalt, antimony, nickel, tin, tungsten, the chip metals gallium and germanium, and the platinum-group metals — alongside an Oil & Gas dashboard (chokepoints, flows, shut-ins, LNG, sanctions, prices and companies) and a Green transition dashboard on the clean-energy supply chain and the EU's green funds. The Mineral Explorer adds production and trade maps for 111 commodities from 1970 to 2024.

Can I download the data behind mineral.watch?

Yes. The open data catalogue lists every dataset: 110 BGS World Mineral Statistics series (production, imports and exports by country, 1970–2024) as JSON files, plus the curated data behind each dashboard. Everything is licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 — cite mineral.watch and the original publisher.

What data sources does mineral.watch use?

Figures are cross-referenced from the USGS National Minerals Information Center, British Geological Survey World Mineral Statistics, UN Comtrade and IEA Critical Minerals data, cited and dated on each page.

Is mineral.watch affiliated with any government or company?

No. mineral.watch is an independent project and holds no commercial position in the minerals, fuels or companies it covers.

Can I commission mineral.watch to produce a report?

Yes — the public dashboards are a sample of what we do. We take commissions for bespoke, source-cited work: supply-chain mapping from mine to refinery to end use, trade-flow and import-dependency analysis, country and commodity deep-dives, custom datasets and interactive dashboards like the ones on this site, and ongoing monitoring briefs for teams in policy, finance, industry and media. We also produce full written reports — charts, maps and citations included — ready for internal circulation or publication. Every deliverable is built on the same verified USGS, BGS, UN Comtrade and IEA data. Email hello@mineral.watch with a short note on the problem you're trying to solve and we'll come back with a scoped proposal.
